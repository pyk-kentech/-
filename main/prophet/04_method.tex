A CD student and the baseline predictor, REL\_GLOBAL, share one
architecture and differ only in the loss used during training. We
first describe REL\_GLOBAL and then the added loss term, the choice of
teachers, the names of the trained groups used in later chapters, and
what was changed when the method was moved to ADS-24 and to three
general encoders.

\section{The baseline predictor}
\label{sec:method-baseline}

REL\_GLOBAL maps the received prefix $X_t$ of
Equation~\ref{eq:receipt} to a distribution over the 18 actions. Each
admitted event is encoded as a 48-dimensional vector, and event-level
gated recurrent units (GRUs)~\cite{cho2014gru} with a 64-dimensional
state advance once per admitted event in receipt order. A relational
residual adds a correction to the action scores, computed over events
that share an observed target key, so that the result recorded for one
target is kept apart from the results of the others. Two regularizers
complete the model. LogitNorm~\cite{wei2022mitigating} computes the cross-entropy on
logits divided by their $L_2$ norm and scaled by a temperature $\tau$,
here $\tau=0.1$; it limits how far the training loss can be lowered by
inflating the logits. Input-gradient regularization~\cite{ross2017improving} penalizes
the gradient of the loss with respect to the input representation,
which discourages predictions that change sharply with small changes
to individual events. Our variant, $\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{GLOBAL}}$,
applies the penalty to events drawn at random from the whole prefix.
The model has 59,882 parameters. This description stays at the level
recorded in the round contracts. They do not document the field layout
of the 48-dimensional event vector, the treatment of UNKNOWN records,
the exact computation of the relational residual and of the output
head, or how $\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{GLOBAL}}$ selects its events, so the
thesis does not by itself give enough detail to reimplement REL\_GLOBAL
(Appendix~\ref{app:repro}).

Training uses the 603 runs of the FIT set
(Section~\ref{sec:conditions}). The objective is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{base}}
  = \mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{FIT}}_{\mathrm{CE}}
  + 0.1\,\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{GLOBAL}},
  \label{eq:base-loss}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{FIT}}_{\mathrm{CE}}$ is the 18-way action
cross-entropy with each FIT run given equal weight. Each model is
trained for 1,346 updates of 32 occurrences with AdamW~\cite{loshchilov2017decoupled}
(weight decay 0.01, gradient clipping at 2), and the learning rate
follows a cosine schedule from 0.002 to 0.0002. The last checkpoint is
kept; no checkpoint is selected by a validation score. All 30 seeds of
a configuration share this procedure and differ only in the seed as
defined in Chapter~\ref{ch:introduction}. The seeds of each round are a
fixed list of integer identifiers written into its contract, not draws
from a stated distribution; Appendix~\ref{app:repro} lists the
remaining training settings.

\section{Evidence for the relational residual}
\label{sec:method-relation-evidence}

The relational residual is kept from earlier models in this project. An
earlier relation model, REL, read target groups through the same kind
of target-key relation path, and it was compared with EVENT GRU, the
same event-level recurrent predictor without any relation path. On 64
new runs (512 rows) from 16 held-out configurations, averaged over
three seeds, the mean NLL was 0.217 for EVENT GRU and 0.046 for REL, and
accuracy rose from 89.1\% to 98.0\%. A control that exchanged target
membership between groups while keeping the group sizes fixed raised
the loss of the relation model from 0.058 to 0.087 on 127 runs (508
rows) that had already been inspected, again with three seeds. The
relation path therefore used which records belong to which target, not
only how many records each group held.

\section{Consensus distillation}
\label{sec:method-cd}

A CD student has the architecture of REL\_GLOBAL and no additional
parameters, so at inference it is the same single model. What changes
is the training loss. Let $J$ be a fixed set of \emph{teachers},
trained REL\_GLOBAL models whose weights are frozen, and let $p_j$ be
the predicted distribution of teacher $j$ at temperature~1. On an
unlabeled input $x$ from the target environment, the target is the
arithmetic mean of the teachers' probabilities, and the student
$p_\theta$ is pulled toward it by a Kullback--Leibler term:
\begin{equation}
  \bar{p}_J(a\mid x)=\frac{1}{|J|}\sum_{j\in J}p_j(a\mid x),
  \qquad
  \mathrm{KL}\bigl(\bar{p}_J \,\|\, p_\theta\bigr)(x)
  =\sum_{a=1}^{18}\bar{p}_J(a\mid x)
   \log\frac{\bar{p}_J(a\mid x)}{p_\theta(a\mid x)} .
  \label{eq:cd-target}
\end{equation}
The divergence runs from the teachers to the student, so the student is
penalized most where the teachers place probability that it does not.
The student's loss keeps both terms of Equation~\ref{eq:base-loss} and
adds this divergence with coefficient 0.25:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}
  = \mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{FIT}}_{\mathrm{CE}}
  + 0.1\,\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{GLOBAL}}
  + 0.25\,\mathrm{KL}\bigl(\bar{p}_J \,\|\, p_\theta\bigr)_{\mathrm{env}} ,
  \label{eq:cd-loss}
\end{equation}
where the subscript denotes the mean over the unlabeled environment
rows of the update. Each update adds 32 such rows to its 32 labeled FIT
occurrences; no label, run identifier, or source and worker
membership of these rows enters the loss. The distillation temperature
is unrelated to the LogitNorm temperature $\tau$: the student still
trains with LogitNorm at $\tau=0.1$, and the teacher target is taken at
temperature~1 without further rescaling. As
Section~\ref{sec:related-distillation} notes, the computation is
knowledge distillation toward the average of several
models~\cite{hinton2015distilling}. Unlike ensemble distribution
distillation~\cite{malinin2019ensemble}, the target is the plain mean
and carries no model of the spread among teachers.

\section{Teacher selection}
\label{sec:method-teachers}

Teachers are chosen from 30 REL\_GLOBAL seeds trained with
Equation~\ref{eq:base-loss}. For each row of the unlabeled A+C inputs,
the 30 models vote with their top-1 actions, and the majority action
serves as the reference; each model's own vote is included. A model's
\emph{top-1 agreement} is the fraction of rows on which its top-1
action equals that majority, computed within each run and then averaged
over runs with equal weight. The five models with the highest agreement
form $J$; they are the teachers of KD\_ENV\_TOP5 and KD\_ALL in the v2
and v3 rounds (Table~\ref{tab:variants}). Section~\ref{sec:related-underspec}
explains how this ranking relates to the agreement estimator of Baek
et al.~\cite{baek2022agreement}.
The rule uses no labels, but it was fixed while we were studying development
runs that had already been inspected, so the teacher choice is not
blind. No round compared it with five teachers chosen at random, with
fewer teachers, or with all 30 as teachers of a student. The results
of later chapters therefore show that CD worked with this teacher rule,
not that ranking by agreement is needed or better than another choice.

Agreement is used to choose teachers, not to choose a deployed model.
The single seed ranked first by agreement failed preservation on
ORIGINAL\_18: on the development runs its NLL was 0.109 above the mean of
the 30 seeds, against the preservation limit of $+0.020$
(Section~\ref{sec:eval-criteria}), while
the average of the top five passed it at the cost of running five models
at inference. Distillation is what returns that five-model target to a
single model. The link between agreement and loss that motivates the
rule was observed in the in-house lab (Chapter~\ref{ch:diagnosis}) and
not on ADS-24 (Chapter~\ref{ch:ads24}).

\section{Transfer to other data and encoders}
\label{sec:method-transfer}

The method was moved to two further settings with as few changes as
possible (Table~\ref{tab:transfer}). On ADS-24, a public dataset of
attack episodes whose host alerts come from the Wazuh security monitor
(Chapter~\ref{ch:ads24}), the input encoder reads Wazuh alert fields, the head predicts nine tactic classes, and the
relational residual is removed; the resulting model has 31,685
parameters. We call it REL\_GLOBAL-ADS to keep it apart from the
in-house REL\_GLOBAL. The three general encoders replace the event encoder with
an LSTM encoder (DeepLog-style)~\cite{hochreiter1997long, du2017deeplog},
a Transformer encoder (LogBERT-style)~\cite{vaswani2017attention, guo2021logbert},
or a plain GRU. They are encoder adaptations to our supervised task, not
reproductions of DeepLog or LogBERT. LogitNorm, input-gradient
regularization, and the relational residual are all removed, and the
width and learning rate of each encoder were chosen by validation inside
the FIT set. Appendix~\ref{app:repro} gives the architectures, the
candidate grid, the validation split, and the selected settings.
