\chapter{Post Hoc Decomposition of Seed Loss}
\label{ch:decomposition}

This chapter is a post hoc analysis: we introduced it after the primary
experiments of Chapters~\ref{ch:results} to~\ref{ch:generality} had
been scored, and none of its results entered any criterion
(Section~\ref{sec:eval-rounds}). It uses only class probabilities that
had already been saved; no model was trained and no execution was
collected for it. For the $S=30$ seeds of one configuration and an input
$x$ with true action $y$, let $p_s$ be the predictive distribution of
seed $s$ and $\tilde p$ their logarithmic opinion pool, the normalized
geometric mean. Then
\begin{equation}
  \frac{1}{S}\sum_{s=1}^{S}\bigl[-\log p_s(y\mid x)\bigr]
  = -\log \tilde p(y\mid x) + V(x),
  \qquad
  \tilde p(a\mid x)\propto
  \exp\Bigl(\frac{1}{S}\sum_{s=1}^{S}\log p_s(a\mid x)\Bigr),
  \qquad
  V(x)=\frac{1}{S}\sum_{s=1}^{S}\mathrm{KL}\bigl(\tilde p\,\|\,p_s\bigr)(x)
  \ge 0 .
  \label{eq:decomposition}
\end{equation}
The mean loss of the seeds is exactly the loss of their consensus plus a
disagreement term $V$, and $V$ depends on the predictions alone, so it
can be computed in the target environment without labels. The
identity is Heskes's result for logarithmic opinion
pools~\cite{heskes1997selecting}, and Gruber and Buettner treat the same
term as the variance component of a bias--variance decomposition for
proper scores~\cite{gruber2022uncertainty}. What we add is only its use
as an unlabeled measure of seed instability under environment shift,
and a reading of which term CD reduces. Like NLL, $V$ is averaged over
the natural boundaries of a run and then over runs with equal weight.
Recomputed from the saved probabilities, the two sides of
Equation~\ref{eq:decomposition} differed by at most about $10^{-15}$.

Table~\ref{tab:decomposition} applies the identity to the baselines of
Chapter~\ref{ch:generality}: the three general encoders and REL\_GLOBAL
on the new PAG1 runs of the in-house lab and on ADS-24. On conditions
absent from FIT, $V$ was three to seven times its value on the seen
condition for every general encoder on both datasets. REL\_GLOBAL-ADS
behaved the same way (5.3 times), but in the in-house lab its
$V$ barely moved (1.1 times). In the in-house lab, disagreement was
about half of the unseen-condition loss for the LSTM and Transformer
encoders and for REL\_GLOBAL, and 70\% for the plain GRU, against 15\%
to 28\% on \texttt{ORIGINAL\_18}. On ADS-24 the share was 7\% to 14\%.
The ADS-24 loss is therefore mostly a loss on which the seeds agree,
which matches Chapter~\ref{ch:ads24}: there the models stay near or
above the NLL of the FIT prior baseline, and lowering $V$ alone cannot
move them far.

$V$ tracked the instability that the earlier chapters measured with the
seed standard deviation of NLL. Over the 28 pairs of encoder and
evaluation condition (four encoders on five in-house conditions and on
two ADS-24 conditions), the Spearman correlation between $V$ and seed standard
deviation was 0.929; it was 0.762 within ADS-24 ($n=8$) and 0.863 within
the in-house lab ($n=20$). The points share seed populations and runs,
so the correlation describes these baselines rather than 28 independent
replications, and we computed no interval for it. The more useful property is that $V$ is available before
labels are. On ADS-24, $V$ measured on the 34 unlabeled adaptation runs
was almost the same as $V$ on the 35 evaluation runs: 0.204 against
0.197 for the LSTM encoder, 0.323 against 0.293 for the Transformer
encoder, 0.148 against 0.149 for the plain GRU, and 0.231 against 0.230
for REL\_GLOBAL-ADS. In the in-house lab the adaptation inputs gave
somewhat lower values for the three general encoders (0.317, 0.455, and
0.700, against 0.339, 0.554, and 0.790).

After distillation, $V$ fell in 27 of the 28 conditions, often by an order
of magnitude: on in-house \texttt{A+C FLAT} it went from 0.796 to 0.011
for the plain GRU, and on the ADS-24 environment evaluation from 0.197
to 0.031 for the LSTM encoder. CD therefore acts mainly on the
disagreement term, which is how it lowers the seed standard deviation.
The exception is REL\_GLOBAL on \texttt{ORIGINAL\_18}, where $V$ rose
from 0.068 to 0.077 between the v3 baseline seeds and the paired CD
student distilled on the A+C inputs alone, and the seed standard
deviation rose with it. Preservation on this condition is also the
least settled in-house result (Section~\ref{sec:disc-mechanism}). The
reduction of unseen-condition spread does not carry over to every
condition.

A small $V$ is not the same as a correct prediction, because the
identity puts the remaining loss into the consensus term. Entropy
minimization, one of the controls of Section~\ref{sec:gen-methods},
drove $V$ on in-house \texttt{A+C FLAT} to essentially zero
($7\times10^{-6}$), and the consensus loss rose to 2.702: the seeds
agreed, and they agreed on the wrong actions. The same can happen in a
milder form after CD. For the plain GRU on the ADS-24 environment
evaluation, $V$ fell from 0.149 to 0.025 while the consensus loss rose
from 2.073 to 2.104; the seed-mean loss improved only because the drop
in $V$ was larger. $V$ is thus a label-free reading of how much the
seeds diverge, and it says nothing on its own about whether their
common answer is right.

Three things the analysis might suggest were not established. First,
$V$ on the unlabeled adaptation inputs did not rank the encoders by
their evaluation seed standard deviation: the Spearman correlation was
$-0.800$ on ADS-24 ($n=4$) and $+0.500$ in the in-house lab ($n=3$), too
few points and inconsistent in sign to support a selection rule.
Second, the decomposition does not make the geometric pool the better
teacher. Scored as teachers, the geometric pool had the lower NLL in
only 24 of 56 same-membership comparisons, and the student comparison
of Section~\ref{sec:gen-methods} did not separate the two pools.
Third, the adaptation-input $V$ of the 30 REL\_GLOBAL seeds of the
in-house lab could not be computed, because only their aggregated
teacher probabilities, not the 30 individual distributions, had been
saved; we did not reconstruct them by re-running the checkpoints.
Related work studies a geometric-mean-based decomposition of
variance-related error under distribution shift with evaluation
labels~\cite{rathmanakidis2022reduction} and uses proper-score
uncertainty for out-of-distribution detection~\cite{gruber2022uncertainty}.
Nothing in our data shows that $V$ measured before deployment predicts
later seed instability.
