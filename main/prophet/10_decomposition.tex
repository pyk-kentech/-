\chapter{Post Hoc Decomposition of Seed Loss}
\label{ch:decomposition}

Everything here is a post hoc analysis: we introduced it after the primary
experiments of Chapters~\ref{ch:results} to~\ref{ch:generality} had
been scored, and none of its results entered any criterion
(Section~\ref{sec:eval-rounds}). It uses only saved class
probabilities. For the $S=30$ seeds of one group and an input
$x$ with true action $y$, let $p_s$ be the predictive distribution of
seed $s$ and $\tilde p$ their logarithmic opinion pool, the normalized
geometric mean. Then
\begin{equation}
  \begin{gathered}
  \frac{1}{S}\sum_{s=1}^{S}\bigl[-\log p_s(y\mid x)\bigr]
  = -\log \tilde p(y\mid x) + V(x),\\
  \tilde p(a\mid x)\propto
  \exp\Bigl(\frac{1}{S}\sum_{s=1}^{S}\log p_s(a\mid x)\Bigr),
  \qquad
  V(x)=\frac{1}{S}\sum_{s=1}^{S}\mathrm{KL}\bigl(\tilde p\,\|\,p_s\bigr)(x)
  \ge 0 .
  \end{gathered}
  \label{eq:decomposition}
\end{equation}
The mean loss of the seeds is exactly the loss of their consensus plus a
disagreement term $V$, and $V$ depends on the predictions alone, so it
can be computed in the target environment without labels; with
$Z(x)$ the normalizer of $\tilde p$, $V(x)=-\log Z(x)$.
% 2026-11-25 분량 줄이기 C17: 항등식 유도 한 줄은 V=−log Z 만 남기고 뺐다(Heskes 가 유도함).
It is the logarithmic-opinion-pool decomposition analyzed by
Heskes~\cite{heskes1997selecting}, and its use as a label-free
uncertainty measure follows Gruber and
Buettner~\cite{gruber2022uncertainty}; Section~\ref{sec:related-decomposition}
states what our computation adds.
% 원문: Heskes NIPS 10 (1997) §2 식 (3): K(q,p̄)=Σ_α w_α K(q,p_α) − Σ_α w_α K(p̄,p_α), K(q,p)=∫q log(q/p),
%   둘째 항은 "minus the logarithm of the normalization constant Z(x)". w_α=1/S, q=정답 한 점으로 두면
%   위 식이 된다. V 의 KL 방향(풀‖seed)은 원문과 같다. Gruber & Buettner AISTATS 2023 §3.4 Corollary 3.6 의
%   B_LSE[ẑ]=E[LSE(ẑ)]−LSE(E[ẑ]) 는 seed logit 에 대해 바로 −log Z(x)=V(x) 이고, §4.2 가 이를 drift 아래
%   OOD 탐지용 불확실성 척도로 쓴다. 확인됨.
Like NLL, $V$ is averaged over
the natural boundaries of a run and then over runs with equal weight.

We applied the identity to the baselines of
Chapter~\ref{ch:generality}, that is, the three general encoders and
REL\_GLOBAL,
on the new PAG1 runs of the in-house lab and on ADS-24
(Table~\ref{tab:decomposition}). On conditions
absent from FIT, $V$ was three to seven times its value on the seen
condition for every general encoder on both datasets. REL\_GLOBAL-ADS
behaved the same way on ADS-24 (5.3 times), whereas the $V$ of the
in-house REL\_GLOBAL barely moved (1.1 times). In the in-house lab, disagreement was
about half of the unseen-condition loss for the LSTM and Transformer
encoders and for REL\_GLOBAL, and 70\% for the plain GRU, against 15\%
to 28\% on \texttt{ORIGINAL\_18}. On ADS-24 the share was 7\% to 14\%.
The ADS-24 loss is therefore mostly a loss on which the seeds agree,
which matches Chapter~\ref{ch:ads24}: there the models stay near or
above the NLL of the FIT prior, and lowering $V$ alone cannot
move them far.

$V$ tracked the instability that the earlier chapters measured with the
seed standard deviation of NLL. Over the 28 model--set pairs (four
models on the five in-house evaluation sets of
Section~\ref{sec:conditions} and on two ADS-24 evaluation sets), the
Spearman correlation between $V$ and seed standard
deviation was 0.929; it was 0.762 within ADS-24 ($n=8$) and 0.863 within
the in-house lab ($n=20$). The points share seeds and runs, so this
describes these baselines, not 28 independent replications (no interval). The more useful property is that $V$ is available before
labels are. On ADS-24, $V$ measured on the 34 unlabeled adaptation runs
was almost the same as $V$ on the 35 evaluation runs: 0.204 against
0.197 for the LSTM encoder, 0.323 against 0.293 for the Transformer
encoder, 0.148 against 0.149 for the plain GRU, and 0.231 against 0.230
for REL\_GLOBAL-ADS. In-house they were somewhat lower for the general
encoders (0.317, 0.455, and 0.700 against 0.339, 0.554, and 0.790).

Each of the same 28 model--set pairs also compares a baseline with the
students of its seed list: for the three general encoders and REL\_GLOBAL-ADS these are
the populations above, while for in-house REL\_GLOBAL they are the v3
baseline seeds and their KD\_ENV\_TOP5 students, scored on the PAG1
runs. After distillation, $V$ fell in 27 of these 28 pairs, often by an order
of magnitude: on in-house \texttt{A+C FLAT} it went from 0.796 to 0.011
for the plain GRU, and on the ADS-24 environment evaluation from 0.197
to 0.031 for the LSTM encoder. This is consistent with CD acting
mainly on the disagreement term, and part of it is expected from
distillation toward one fixed teacher set
(Section~\ref{sec:disc-novelty}). The ensemble analogue of this
reduction is shown by Gruber and Buettner: averaging members over
initializations shrinks the Bregman Information that this randomness
contributes, and with it the expected
loss~\cite{gruber2022uncertainty}.
% 원문: Gruber & Buettner AISTATS 2023 §3.5 Prop. 3.8 식 (1)–(3), 식 (4)–(5) pp.6–7: W(초기값)에 대한 조건부
%   Bregman Information 이 앙상블 크기와 함께 줄고 n→∞ 에서 0, "the expected negative log-likelihood reduces"
%   by that amount. (옛 인용 rathmanakidis2022reduction 을 바꿈.)
The decomposition is an identity and
the change in $V$ is an observation after the fact, so the analysis
does not show that the drop in $V$ produced the drop in seed standard
deviation.

The exception is REL\_GLOBAL on \texttt{ORIGINAL\_18}. Between the v3
baseline seeds and their paired KD\_ENV\_TOP5 students, distilled on the
background inputs alone, $V$ rose from 0.068 to 0.077, and the seed
standard deviation rose with it. These v3 seeds are not the
REL\_GLOBAL population of Table~\ref{tab:decomposition}.

A small $V$ is not the same as a correct prediction, because the
identity puts the remaining loss into the consensus term. Entropy
minimization, one of the controls of Section~\ref{sec:gen-methods},
drove $V$ on in-house \texttt{A+C FLAT} to essentially zero
($7\times10^{-6}$), and the consensus loss rose to 2.702: the seeds
agreed, and they agreed on the wrong actions. The same can happen in a
milder form after CD. For the plain GRU on the ADS-24 environment
evaluation, $V$ fell from 0.149 to 0.025 while the consensus loss rose
from 2.073 to 2.104; the seed-mean loss improved only because the drop
in $V$ was larger. $V$ thus reads how much the seeds diverge, not whether
their common answer is right.
